SMOOTH AS REMOVAL ROUGH AS MARKING

At the literal level, smooth and rough operate as expected opposites across surfaces, materials, and physical contact.

In metaphorical uses, the opposition becomes asymmetric. Smooth tends to describe friction, interruption, or irregularity after it has been reduced: a smooth operation, transition, or performance. Rough more often makes a residual condition explicit: a rough passage, time, draft, or estimate.

Shared nouns show that the contrast belongs to context rather than to the noun alone. Their horizontal position records the balance between smooth and rough frequency; size records their combined frequency.

Smooth presents the condition after friction is reduced. Rough marks what remains unresolved.

This is a pattern in this dataset, not a universal definition.
